# Reflection

## What did I learn today?

- Working with AI is not "type one prompt and get the perfect result." It is more like a back-and-forth conversation.
- The best results came when I actually **checked** the AI's work, not when I wrote a longer or fancier prompt.
  - For the AI Challenge task, I looked at real screenshots of the app and compared them to what I wanted, instead of just trusting the description of what was built.
  - For the Product Analysis task, I checked the diagram content directly to make sure all 4 questions were actually answered, not just assumed.
- Giving exact details helps a lot. When I gave exact colors, spacing, and icon names, the result matched much better than when I described things loosely.
- AI is good at doing the repetitive work fast (setting up files, writing similar components, following a pattern I already showed it). My job was to decide *what* to build and *check if it was right*, not to write every line myself.
- For the Product Analysis task, I also learned that picking MVP features is not about picking the "coolest" ones — it's about picking the ones everything else depends on.

## What challenged me the most?

- Keeping the AI's work inside the scope I actually asked for.
  - A few times, it built more than I asked (extra pages I didn't request, a dependency installed "just in case").
  - A few times, it built less carefully than I asked (using close-enough colors instead of the exact ones I gave).
- Both problems needed the same fix: I had to **notice** the mismatch, say clearly what was wrong, and wait for the correction.
- The hard part wasn't writing prompts. The hard part was staying alert enough to catch small drifts in scope or accuracy before they piled up.
- Deciding when to trust the AI's suggestion and when to reject it also took thought each time — I kept the suggestions that solved a real repeated problem (like pulling repeated code into one shared component), and rejected the ones that added things I never asked for.

## If I had another hour, what would I improve?

- I would spend more time reviewing before accepting work, instead of after — catching mismatches earlier means fewer correction rounds later.
- I would write down my exact requirements (colors, scope, what's included/excluded) before starting, instead of adding them after seeing the first draft.
- I would double check every AI suggestion against "did I actually ask for this?" before letting it stay in the project.
- For the Product Analysis task, I would keep the diagram and the written summary in sync more carefully so they never say two different things.
